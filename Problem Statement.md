# Problem Statement

## 1. Problem Overview

There is a significant and persistent gap between technology education and employment.

Students and fresh graduates can spend years studying Computer Science, Software Engineering, Information Systems, Artificial Intelligence, and related disciplines, yet still enter the job market without the practical experience required to secure their first professional opportunity.

The problem is not simply a lack of technical knowledge.

The problem is the lack of access to structured, meaningful, and verifiable professional experience.

Students need opportunities to work in teams, contribute to real projects, follow professional development workflows, receive expert feedback, and understand how software is actually built inside a professional environment.

However, the traditional internship ecosystem is not sufficiently scalable or consistent to meet this demand.

This creates an Experience Gap between learning and employment.

---

# 2. The Student & Graduate Problem

The number of students, fresh graduates, and technology learners seeking internships significantly exceeds the number of meaningful opportunities available to them.

Students from fields such as:

- Computer Science
- Information Systems
- Software Engineering
- Artificial Intelligence
- Computer Engineering
- Related technology disciplines

often struggle to secure their first internship or practical opportunity.

When students cannot access meaningful internships, they commonly rely on alternatives such as:

- Online courses
- Recorded tutorials
- Personal projects
- Academic projects
- Certificates
- Unstructured training programs

Although these activities can provide knowledge, they do not necessarily provide professional experience.

A student may understand a technology but still lack experience with:

- Working within a software team
- Git and collaborative development
- Agile workflows
- Sprint planning
- Daily standups
- Code reviews
- Pull requests
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Professional communication
- Project delivery
- Working under deadlines

As a result, many graduates reach the job market with knowledge but without sufficient evidence that they can operate effectively in a professional environment.

---

# 3. The Internship Availability Problem

The demand for internships is significantly higher than the capacity of traditional companies to provide them.

This problem was directly observed through the team's own internship operation.

A simple internship opportunity published by the company's existing software operation received approximately:

**10,000 applications within 48 hours.**

No major sponsorship or large-scale advertising campaign was required to generate this level of interest.

Only approximately 200 applicants could be accommodated.

This demonstrates the significant imbalance between:

**Demand for practical experience**

and

**Available internship capacity.**

The large number of applicants also indicates that many students and graduates are actively searching for opportunities to gain practical experience and enter the technology industry.

---

# 4. The Quality Problem

Availability is only one part of the problem.

Even when an internship opportunity exists, the quality and structure of the experience can vary significantly.

Many training opportunities do not provide a complete representation of professional software development.

Students may receive:

- Individual assignments
- Basic tutorials
- Small isolated projects
- Limited mentor interaction
- Minimal teamwork
- Attendance-based certificates

This creates a gap between completing an internship and actually experiencing professional software development.

A meaningful internship should expose participants to the processes, collaboration, responsibilities, and challenges that exist inside a real software organization.

---

# 5. The Company Problem

Companies face a different side of the same problem.

When hiring junior talent, organizations often receive a large number of applications while having limited information about the candidate's actual capabilities.

A traditional CV can show:

- Education
- Courses
- Certifications
- Self-reported skills
- Previous experience

However, it does not always provide reliable evidence of how a candidate performs in a collaborative software environment.

Companies therefore need to invest significant time and resources into:

- CV screening
- Technical assessments
- Interviews
- Behavioral interviews
- Candidate evaluation
- Onboarding
- Training

There is also a significant hiring risk.

A candidate may appear qualified on paper but require substantial training after joining the company.

This increases:

- Hiring costs
- Training costs
- Time-to-productivity
- Recruitment workload
- Hiring uncertainty

---

# 6. The University Problem

Universities face another dimension of the same challenge.

Students increasingly need practical and industrial training as part of their academic journey and, in many cases, graduation requirements.

However, universities often depend on external companies and limited internship partnerships to provide these opportunities.

This creates several challenges.

### Limited Capacity

Partner companies may not be able to accommodate the full number of students requiring training.

### Inconsistent Training Quality

Students may be distributed across different organizations with different training standards and experiences.

### Limited Visibility

Universities may have difficulty continuously monitoring:

- Attendance
- Training hours
- Student performance
- Project participation
- Mentor feedback
- Training completion

### Verification

It can also be difficult to consistently verify whether students actually received meaningful practical training.

As a result, universities need a more scalable, structured, measurable, and verifiable approach to practical training.

---

# 7. The Core Experience Gap

The problems faced by students, companies, and universities are interconnected.

Students need experience to become employable.

Companies need evidence of capability before hiring.

Universities need reliable ways to provide and verify practical training.

However, the current ecosystem does not efficiently connect these three needs.

This creates the central problem:

> **Students are expected to have experience before they can obtain the opportunities required to gain that experience.**

The result is a cycle:

Learning
↓
Graduation
↓
Lack of Experience
↓
Difficulty Getting Internship
↓
Difficulty Getting First Job
↓
Continued Experience Gap

---

# 8. Root Cause

The root cause is not simply a shortage of educational content.

There is already an abundance of courses, tutorials, bootcamps, and educational resources available to technology learners.

The deeper issue is the lack of a scalable system that converts learning into verified professional experience.

Students need a bridge between:

**Learning**

and

**Working.**

That bridge must provide more than educational content.

It must provide structured experience, professional collaboration, mentorship, measurable performance, and credible verification.

---

# 9. Problem Summary

The market currently faces a three-sided experience gap:

### Students & Graduates

Need practical experience but struggle to access meaningful internships.

### Companies

Need job-ready junior talent but struggle to identify and evaluate candidates based on real capabilities.

### Universities

Need scalable, structured, and verifiable practical training opportunities for their students.

These problems reinforce each other and create a persistent gap between education and employment.

The fundamental problem can therefore be summarized as:

> **There is no sufficiently scalable and structured bridge that allows students and fresh graduates to gain verified real-world professional experience before entering the job market, while simultaneously helping universities provide reliable practical training and helping companies identify genuinely job-ready talent.**
